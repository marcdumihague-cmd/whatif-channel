# What If You Were the Last Liar on Earth?

Title: **What If You Were the Last Liar on Earth?** · Thumbnail text: **$1,000,000**
(runner-up title: What If 8 Billion People Believed Every Word You Said?)

Status: draft script, waiting for the user's approval. Nothing produced yet.

Cast (voices to design): Narrator (existing), You (existing), Theo (best friend, wannabe
comedian), plus small parts: Barista, Weatherman, Dana (boss), Poker player, Pit boss, Police
officer, Flight attendant, Chef, Car salesman, HR manager, Pop star, Snack CEO, Audience member.

Running gag: Theo, "Be honest. Am I funny?" (3 times, flipped at the end).
Object that carries the plot: the **LIES TOLD** counter, top-left, ticking up all video.
Planted mystery: "what one lie at a party will cost" (paid off at 6:00).
Hidden goal: Theo is about to quit comedy, and you are the only person left who can say "you were amazing".

---

## 1. Cold open (0:00)

**NARRATOR:** At 3 a.m., you became the last liar on Earth.
**NARRATOR:** By lunch, you've won 2,000 dollars at poker.
**NARRATOR:** But nobody warns you what one lie at a party will cost.

[ON SCREEN: Night bedroom, rain on the window, clock 3:00. Camera pulls out through the window to
the whole planet at night; a soft wave of light ripples across it, skipping one tiny dot: you.
Counter pops in top-left: LIES TOLD: 0.]

## 2. The first lie (0:15)

**NARRATOR:** Over the next 6 weeks, you'll make a million dollars. And you'll almost lose the one person who matters.

[ON SCREEN: Quick flash-forward collage in thought bubbles: a pile of cash, a TV studio, a line of people in the rain, a friend walking away. Then snap back to black.]

[CHIP: 7:40 A.M.]

**NARRATOR:** You don't know yet. You just want coffee.
**BARISTA:** Morning! Honestly, the coffee's burnt today. I burnt it.
**BARISTA:** Also, I don't like your jacket.

[ON SCREEN: Coffee shop, steam loops off the cups. Barista smiles while saying it. Punch-in zoom on
"I burnt it". Your face: one eyebrow up.]

**NARRATOR:** On the TV behind her, the weatherman looks like he hasn't slept.
**WEATHERMAN:** Will it rain tomorrow? I have no idea. Nobody does. We guess.

[ON SCREEN: TV in the corner, weather map with a giant question mark spinning.]

**NARRATOR:** You laugh, and say the first dumb thing that comes to mind.
**YOU:** I'm the manager. This one's on the house.
**BARISTA:** Oh! Sorry! On the house.
**NARRATOR:** She believes you. Instantly. Completely.

[ON SCREEN: Free cup slides across the counter. Counter ticks: LIES TOLD: 1, with a little "ding".]

## 3. The rules (0:50)

**NARRATOR:** Here's the thing. You were never good at spotting liars. Nobody is.
**NARRATOR:** In studies, people catch a lie about 54 percent of the time. Barely better than flipping a coin.

[ON SCREEN: A giant coin flips in slow motion. Pop-up stat: 54%.]

**NARRATOR:** Overnight, everyone else lost the ability to say anything they believe is false.
No lies. No bluffs. No "I'm fine."
**NARRATOR:** And because lies don't exist anymore, nobody expects one. Whatever you say, they believe.

[ON SCREEN: Thought bubble with three icons popping in one by one, each crossed out: a speech
bubble with a fib, a poker face, a forced smile saying "I'm fine".]

**NARRATOR:** The average person tells one or two lies a day. Today, the entire planet's total is... you.

[ON SCREEN: World counter: 8,000,000,000 PEOPLE / LIES TODAY: 1. Zoom on the 1.]

## 4. Theo (1:25)

[ON SCREEN: Split screen phone call. Theo in his messy room, joke notebook in hand.]

**THEO:** Dude. Everyone's being so weird. My mom said my comedy is "not funny, but brave."
**NARRATOR:** This is Theo. Your best friend since third grade. He's wanted to be a stand-up comedian for ten years.
**NARRATOR:** He is not good.
**THEO:** Be honest. Am I funny?
**NARRATOR:** And for the first time, you realize you're the only person on Earth who can answer that however you want.
**YOU:** ...I gotta go.

[ON SCREEN: Thought bubble from Theo's head: tiny Theo on a stage, a single cricket chirping.
Slow push-in on your face. Hold one beat of silence.]

**NARRATOR:** Then your boss calls.
**YOU:** Hi Dana. I'm sick today.
**DANA:** Oh no! Honestly, I was going to fire you today. Feel better!

[ON SCREEN: Counter: LIES TOLD: 2. Your face freezes mid-cough.]

**NARRATOR:** You're not sick. You are, apparently, about to be fired. You have bigger plans.

## 5. Money exploit #1: poker (2:05)

[CHIP: 9:00 A.M.]

**NARRATOR:** You open an online poker game.
**NARRATOR:** Bluffing is just lying with chips. And nobody else can do it.
**NARRATOR:** When they have a good hand, they bet. When they don't, they fold. They might as well be playing with their cards face up.

[ON SCREEN: Poker table seen from above. The other players' cards flip face up with a "pop".
Glowing arrows from each hand to a sign: BET or FOLD.]

**NARRATOR:** You go all in with a seven and a two. The worst hand in poker.
**POKER PLAYER:** He must have something. I fold.

[ON SCREEN: Chips slide to you. Counter: LIES TOLD: 3. Money counter pops in top-right: $2,000.]

**NARRATOR:** By lunch, you're up two thousand dollars. By Thursday, you're in Las Vegas.

[CHIP: THURSDAY · LAS VEGAS. Neon signs flicker, slot machines spin in the background, the lie
counter starts spinning like a slot reel: 3... 47... 300...]

**NARRATOR:** Fun fact: counting cards isn't illegal in Las Vegas. But casinos can still ban you for being too good.
**NARRATOR:** You last six hours.
**PIT BOSS:** Sir. Honestly? You scare me. Please leave.

[ON SCREEN: Two security guards walk you out, your arms full of chips. Money: $41,000.]

**NARRATOR:** And the small stuff works too.
**YOU:** Officer, I'm late for my own wedding.
**POLICE OFFICER:** Congratulations! Go, go!
**YOU:** It's our honeymoon.
**FLIGHT ATTENDANT:** First class. On us.
**YOU:** I'm a food critic.
**CHEF:** ...Everything is free. Please be kind.

[ON SCREEN: Fast montage, one shot per line, each ending with a counter tick and a "ding": flashing
lights in a rearview mirror, a plane seat upgrade, a chef sweating over a plate.]

## 6. Money exploit #2: the secret number (3:25)

**NARRATOR:** Poker made you 41,000 dollars. But you're about to make 10 times that without touching a single card.
**NARRATOR:** Every deal has a secret number. The most the buyer will pay. The least the seller will take.
**NARRATOR:** Nobody can hide theirs anymore.

[ON SCREEN: Two people across a table; above each head a hidden number, then both numbers pop
into view.]

**NARRATOR:** Your car is dying, so you visit a dealership.
**CAR SALESMAN:** This one's twenty-four thousand. Honestly, my boss lets me go down to nineteen. And the brakes squeak.
**YOU:** Seventeen. Final offer. Another dealer's waiting for me.
**NARRATOR:** There is no other dealer.
**CAR SALESMAN:** ...Seventeen it is.

[ON SCREEN: Price tag on the windshield: 24,000 crossed out, 19,000 crossed out, 17,000 stamped.
Counter ticks.]

**NARRATOR:** Psychologists have found that the first number on the table drags the final price toward it. They call it anchoring.
**NARRATOR:** And you're now the only person on Earth who can drop a fake anchor.

[ON SCREEN: A cartoon anchor crashes down onto a price tag. This is the one bar chart moment of the
section: three bars, "his ask / his real limit / your price".]

**NARRATOR:** So you start a business. Negotiator for hire. Salaries, houses, contracts. You take ten percent.
**HR MANAGER:** The budget for this job is ninety thousand. Honestly, we'd go to a hundred and ten.
**YOU:** My client already has an offer for a hundred and twenty.
**NARRATOR:** Your client gets a hundred and fifteen. You get eleven thousand five hundred dollars. For one phone call.

[CHIP: WEEK 3. Money: $380,000.]

## 7. Money exploit #3: Just Asking (4:35)

**NARRATOR:** Then you have the idea that makes you a millionaire in 6 weeks. And it almost costs you your best friend.
**NARRATOR:** If nobody can lie, every famous person on Earth has a problem. Because someone can just... ask.

[ON SCREEN: Title card for your show, "JUST ASKING", bold letters on a dark gradient, a dotted
line pulsing under it. Studio lights sweep.]

**NARRATOR:** Episode one: a pop star.
**YOU:** Did you write your own songs?
**POP STAR:** ...No. A man named Gary writes all of them. I've never met Gary.

[ON SCREEN: Inset photo pops in: a shadowy silhouette labeled "GARY?".]

**NARRATOR:** Episode two: the boss of a snack company.
**YOU:** Is your new chip healthy?
**SNACK CEO:** It's mostly salt and regret.
**NARRATOR:** Technically, they're allowed to stay silent. But on live TV, silence is an answer too.

[ON SCREEN: A guest sweating in total silence while a clock ticks; the audience slowly leans in.]

**NARRATOR:** Ten million views. Then forty million. Sponsors line up, and every one of them has to tell you exactly how much they're willing to pay.
**NARRATOR:** Six weeks in, you're a millionaire.

[CHIP: WEEK 6. View counter rolling up, money counter hits $1,000,000 with a burst of confetti.]

**NARRATOR:** Theo comes to watch a taping.
**THEO:** Be honest. Can I do the warm-up act?
**NARRATOR:** You let him. It does not go well.
**AUDIENCE MEMBER:** I'm not laughing because it's not funny.
**NARRATOR:** Theo laughs it off. But you notice he stops carrying his joke notebook.

[ON SCREEN: Theo on the small warm-up stage, the audience faces politely blank. Afterwards, slow
zoom as he pushes the notebook deep into his pocket. Music drops out.]

## 8. The setback: one lie at a party (5:55)

**NARRATOR:** That night, you tell one lie as a joke. By 2 a.m., half the city is about to lose its money.
**NARRATOR:** Someone at a party asks what you think about Harbor Bank. Half the city keeps their money there.
**YOU:** Harbor Bank? It's going under. Get your money out tonight.
**NARRATOR:** Everyone at the party believes you. Of course they do.
**NARRATOR:** Then they text their friends. Who also can't imagine anyone would lie.

[ON SCREEN: One phone lights up, then ten, then a hundred, spreading across a night city map like
the light wave from the opening. Counter ticks.]

**NARRATOR:** And this part isn't fiction. In 2023, customers of a real bank, Silicon Valley Bank, tried to pull out 42 billion dollars in a single day. The next morning, regulators shut it down.
**NARRATOR:** A bank doesn't have to be broken to fail. It just needs enough people to believe it is.

[ON SCREEN: Real-fact card: "Silicon Valley Bank, March 2023: $42 billion requested in one day".
A line chart falling off a cliff.]

**NARRATOR:** By 2 a.m., there's a line around the block. In the rain.

[ON SCREEN: Rain, streetlights, a long line of people with umbrellas outside the bank, ATM glow.]

**NARRATOR:** So you go live on your own show.
**YOU:** I made it up. It was a joke. Harbor Bank is fine.
**NARRATOR:** That one's true. They believe that too. The line goes home.

**NARRATOR:** But Theo was at that party. And Theo isn't stupid.
**THEO:** Wait. That was a lie. You can lie.
**YOU:** ...Yeah.
**THEO:** Since when?
**YOU:** Since everyone else stopped.
**THEO:** So every time I asked if I was funny...
**NARRATOR:** You never answered. Not once. And that was its own answer.

[ON SCREEN: Two-shot in your apartment, rain on the window behind you. Close-ups alternate;
Theo's eyes go from hurt to quiet. He leaves. The door clicks.]

## 9. The real cost (7:10)

**NARRATOR:** Here's what nobody tells you about a world without lies: you'd never hear "you look great" again. Not once. It's lonely.
**NARRATOR:** A lot of everyday lies aren't selfish at all. They're told to protect someone else. "I love it." "You look great." "You were amazing up there."
**NARRATOR:** All of that is gone. From everyone. Except you.

[ON SCREEN: Muted montage, rain in every window: a friend shows a haircut, "it's worse"; a kid
holds up a drawing, "it's not good"; a grandma's cooking, "it's dry". Colors slowly desaturate.]

**NARRATOR:** A week later, Theo calls.
**THEO:** I'm doing one last set. Open mic, Friday. After that, I'm done. For real.

## 10. Climax (7:45)

[CHIP: FRIDAY · 10:15 P.M.]

**NARRATOR:** A basement comedy club. Forty people. Theo walks out.
**NARRATOR:** And he's... okay. Not great. But there's one joke, about his mom calling his comedy "brave," that gets a real laugh.
**NARRATOR:** Forty honest people. One real laugh. That's not nothing.

[ON SCREEN: One spotlight, Theo sweating. A wall of blank faces, then a single "HA!" bubble pops
up from the crowd. Theo blinks.]

**NARRATOR:** After the show, he finds you by the door.
**THEO:** Be honest. Was it good?
**NARRATOR:** You're the only person on Earth who gets to choose the answer.
**NARRATOR:** You could tell him the truth. Three out of ten. One great joke.
**NARRATOR:** Or you could say the one thing nobody else is allowed to say anymore.
**YOU:** It was the best set I've ever seen.

[ON SCREEN: Slow push-in, music swells. The counter ticks one more time, and this time the number
glows warm instead of red.]

**NARRATOR:** He knows you can lie. He looks at you for a long time.
**THEO:** Thanks. I needed someone to say it.
**NARRATOR:** And he keeps going.

[ON SCREEN: Outside, the rain stops.]

## 11. Bookend and coda (8:35)

[CHIP: ONE YEAR LATER · 3:00 A.M.]

**NARRATOR:** A year later, Theo has a real show. Two hundred seats. Most of them laugh.
**NARRATOR:** At 3 a.m., your phone buzzes. It's Theo.
**THEO:** Remember the night you said I was amazing?
**YOU:** Yeah.
**THEO:** Honestly? I was terrible.
**YOU:** Honestly? You were.
**NARRATOR:** You both laugh until four.

[ON SCREEN: Same bedroom as the opening, same rain, same 3:00 clock, but now you're grinning at your
phone.]

**NARRATOR:** You still have the show. You still win poker night, every Thursday.
**NARRATOR:** And you only lie when it's kind. Mostly.

[ON SCREEN: Counter spins to a huge number and stops; a tiny wink.]

**NARRATOR:** So, if you were the last liar on Earth, what would you do first? Tell us in the comments, and subscribe.

[ON SCREEN: End card, title on dark gradient, dotted line pulsing.]
