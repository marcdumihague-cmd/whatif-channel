# What If You Could Call Anyone Who Died for 60 Seconds?

Status: script written to `youtube/viral-notes.md`; generated from `script.json` (the source of truth).

Hook (scored 76, STRONG): "Your grandpa died 9 days ago, but at 3 a.m. he picks up the phone, and you have 60 seconds before you lose him forever."

How it applies the rules: emotional cold open + open loop (Grandpa's unfinished sentence, paid at ~85%); goal + deadline (save the house in 30 days, 30 calls); money only as a means; real checkable details (Einstein, Netflix/Blockbuster, $70B unclaimed, $30B in bonds); planted odd detail (three taps), paid at the climax; a sceptic who converts (Mom); a running gag that flips (Theo's "Can you call Elvis?" becomes "...my dad?"); bookend (3:07 a.m.); one question to the viewer.


## cold_open

**NARRATOR:** Your grandpa died 9 days ago.
**NARRATOR:** But at 3 a.m., he picks up the phone. And you have 60 seconds before you lose him forever.
**GRANDPA:** Kiddo? Is that you?
**YOU:** Grandpa?!
**GRANDPA:** Listen. I don't have long. Don't let them sell the house. The blue tin. Second drawer. Tell your grandma I...
**NARRATOR:** Click. The line goes dead. Exactly 60 seconds.

## promise

**NARRATOR:** Over the next 30 days, you'll talk to Albert Einstein, make 86,000 dollars, and find out how that sentence ends.

## rules

**NARRATOR:** Here's how it works. It's Grandpa's old rotary phone. Dial anyone who has ever died, and they pick up.
**NARRATOR:** You get exactly 60 seconds. One call a day. And every person only once.
**NARRATOR:** Grandpa already used his.
**NARRATOR:** More than 100 billion people have ever lived. You get to choose 30.

## grandma

**NARRATOR:** The next morning, you go to Grandma's.
**GRANDMA:** The bank wants 86,000 dollars by the end of the month. Or the house goes.
**NARRATOR:** The house. With the blue tin inside.
**GRANDMA:** It's only a house, sweetheart. Fifty-one years, and he never once said he loved me.
**NARRATOR:** Grandpa tapped the table three times before every dinner. Nobody ever asked him why.
**GRANDMA:** I talked to him all night at the hospital. I don't know if he heard a word.
**NARRATOR:** In 2020, scientists recorded the brains of dying hospice patients. Even the ones who couldn't respond still reacted to sound. Hearing may be one of the last things to go.

## einstein
